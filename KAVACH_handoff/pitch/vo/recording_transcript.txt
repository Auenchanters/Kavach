Every second in India, someone falls victim to cyber fraud. In 2025 alone, India's 1930 helpline received 32.4 million calls, with 22,931 crore rupees lost across 28 lakh reported cases. And 3 out of 4 rupees were never recovered. Over 1 lakh senior citizens lost 4,005 crore rupees. Awareness campaigns fail when fear takes over. Caller ID apps can warn you, but they don't act for you once the money is gone. And official helplines expect panicked victims to act within a single golden hour. Nobody stands between your parents and the scammer during the call, or acts for them right after. That is why we built Kavach. For wackiest 2026, track 1. Make MCP Matter. Kavach is a voice-first multi-agent AI system for ordinary families. Elderly parents like Amma and guardians like her son Rahul. Four layers of agents, shield, strike, recover and intelligence, connect through MCP to the channels families already use, and to India's official fraud systems. First, shield runs every day. Saved contacts ring normally, while risky unknown calls are screened first. If Amma picks up a suspicious call, she taps one large Kavach button. Kavach joins as a three-way call, detects scam patterns live, and warns her calmly in Kannada, Hindi or English. Rahul is alerted on Telegram. Suspicious SMS and WhatsApp links are checked too, with a voice note in reply. Second, strike fights back. Forwarded scam calls route to a decoy AI retiree, using strictly fake details. It keeps scammers talking, draws out UPI IDs in new accounts, and drafts reports for Sancharsati, Chukshu and banks. Third, recover races the golden hour. Triggered by one tap or a debit SMS, four agents launch in parallel. A bank block request, a 1930 briefing, a pre-filled cybercrime complaint, and a time-stamped evidence pack, all waiting on Rahul's one-tap approval. Fourth, intelligence. Every confirmed scam number, URL and UPI ID becomes a node in a shared fraud graph, exposing whole scam rings, so one family's attack protects thousands. Technically, Kavach isn't a chatbot with bolted-on plugins. It's a modular, MCP-first agent system. At the top, 11 Labs agents handle speech, turn-taking, and multilingual voice, talking to our MCP layer over streamable HTTP. Under it sits the Kavach orchestrator, in Python and FastAPI, with Gemini Flash scoring scams across our Shield, Strike, Recover, and Guardian agents. Agents never talk to outside systems directly. Every tool call passes through a policy gate, with an audit log and three action tiers, automatic for screening and warnings. Guardian approved through Telegram for reports or bank holds, and never allowed, sharing real data, moving money, or posing as officials. Below the gate sits six fast MCP servers, telephony, Intel, bank, report, family, and knowledge, plus a live fraud graph and a Guardian dashboard, so future bank or telecom agents can plug into the same governance layer. Kavach never uses Android accessibility permissions, and never reads your banking screens. That is exactly how malware steals OTPs. It relies only on consent-based channels, announced conference calls, debit SMS, and RBI's account aggregator, aligned with India's DPDP Act. In our build, voice agents, scam detection, all six MCP servers, SMS passing, Telegram approvals, and the fraud graph are real. Only bank actions and official portal filings are mocked.
